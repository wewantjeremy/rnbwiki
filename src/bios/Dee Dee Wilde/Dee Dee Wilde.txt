	Dee Dee Wilde, born Diane Wynese Williams is from Miami and is the sister of Eugene Wilde Al Broomfield. She started recording with her siblings, appearing in family groups including Tight Connection, Life, La Voyage and Simplicious. Simplicious had the 1984 R&B record “Let Her Feel It.”
	Eugene Wilde's success helped Dee Dee land a deal with 4th & Broadway. Her first solo single, “I Found You,” came out in 1988 and reached #98 in the UK. Her first album, No Way Out, was released only in Europe and Japan, which helps explain why she's obscure to American R&B listeners despite being American. She co-wrote and co-produced the material, credited as D. Williams. The title track wasn't completely invisible either: “No Way Out” was #5 on Music Week's UK dance chart in June 1989.
	In 1989 she also sang on George Howard's Personal and appeared with Urban High on a version of Loleatta Holloway's “Runaway.” In 1992 she released Get-A-Way. The title track reached #60 R&B in the US. The album might have been her American breakthrough, but Northern Star ran into business problems and folded before the campaign could really go anywhere.
That same material was subsequently issued in the UK as I Love You on Trans-A.
	Dee Dee Wilde was also a legitimate session vocalist, with credits including Phyllis Hyman, James Brown, Donna Allen, INXS, Gerald Alston, Chris Walker and Will to Power. She's credited on Hyman's Prime of My Life, for example.
	Around 2001, she changed the styling to DeDee Wilde and released PG-13 through the family's Wilde City label, with Eugene involved in writing/production.
