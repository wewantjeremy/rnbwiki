	Glenn Jones (born on September 27, 1962 in Jacksonville, Florida) is an R&B singer who scored various hits on the R&B charts throughout the ’80s and ’90s.
	Glenn began his career as a gospel singer with a gospel music group in Florida called The Modulations before switching to R&B music.
	He made his R&B debut in 1980 when he was featured on Norman Connors’ song, “Melancholy Fire” on his album, “Take It to the Limit” which peaked at #20 on Billboard’s Hot Soul Singles chart. He would later tour with Connors. In 1983, he signed a record deal with RCA Records and released his debut five-track mini-album called, “Everybody Loves a Winner.” The lead single, “I Am Somebody” peaked at #30 on Billboard’s Hot Soul Singles chart (staying on the chart for 14 weeks) and #43 on Billboard’s Dance/Disco Top 80 chart (staying on the chart for 8 weeks)
	The follow-up single, “Keep On Doin'” peaked at #51 on Billboard’s Hot Soul Singles chart, staying on the chart for 8 weeks. In 1984, Glenn released his second and first full-length album, “Finesse” which peaked at #18 on Billboard’s R&B Albums chart. The first single from the album, “Show Me” peaked at #3 on Billboard’s Hot Soul Singles chart, staying on the chart for 23 weeks. The follow-up singles, “Bring Back Your Love” (which peaked at #18) and “Everlasting Love” (which peaked at #85) were moderately successful on the R&B charts. In 1985, Glenn made the R&B charts with singer Dionne Warwick with their song, “Finder of Lost Loves” which peaked at #47 on Billboard’s Hot Black Singles chart, staying on the chart for 10 weeks. In 1986, he released his third album, “Take It From Me” which peaked at #45 on Billboard’s R&B Albums chart. The first single from the album, “Giving Myself to You” peaked at #19 on Billboard’s Hot Black Singles chart, staying on the chart for 15 weeks.
	The follow-up single, “Stay” peaked at #69 on Billboard’s Hot Black Singles chart (staying on the chart for 17 weeks). Another track from the album, the title song was featured in the 1986 film, “Youngblood.” During that same year, he was featured on singer & future wife Genobia Jeter’s song, “Together” which peaked at #42 on Billboard’s Hot Black Singles chart (staying on the chart for 10 weeks). In 1987, Glenn moved from RCA Records to Jive Records. During that same year, he released his fourth self-titled album which peaked at #94 on the Billboard 200, #16 on Billboard’s R&B Albums chart and #62 on the UK Albums chart. The lead single, “We’ve Only Just Begun (The Romance Is Not Over”) peaked at #2 on Billboard’s Hot Black Singles chart, staying on the chart for 20 weeks. It also crossed over to the pop music charts where it peaked at #66 on the Billboard Hot 100 and peaked at #85 on the UK Singles chart.
	The follow-up singles, a cover version of the Chi-Lites’ song, “Oh Girl” (which peaked at #38) and “Living in the Limelight” (which peaked at #34) were moderately successful on the R&B charts. By the ’90s, Glenn had switched record labels again, this time to Atlantic Records. In 1990, he released his fifth album, “All For You” which peaked at #27 on Billboard’s R&B Albums chart. The first single, “Stay” (not to be confused with the track of the same name from his 1986 album) peaked at #6 on Billboard’s Hot Black Singles chart, staying on the chart for 17 weeks. The song would later be covered by girl group Eternal in 1993, becoming a worldwide hit for them.
	The follow-up single, “Can We Try Again” peaked at #47 on Billboard’s Hot Black Singles chart, staying on the chart for 10 weeks.
	In April of 1992, Glenn scored his first & only number-one R&B song to date, “Here I Go Again” which topped Billboard’s Hot R&B Singles chart for a week, staying on the charts for 19 weeks. During that same year, he released his sixth album, “Here I Go Again” which peaked at #22 on Billboard’s R&B Albums chart. The follow-up single, “I’ve Been Searchin’ (Nobody Like You)” peaked at #8 on Billboard’s Hot R&B Singles charts, staying on the charts for 30 weeks, making it his longest charting single to date.
	The last charting single from the album, “Good Thang” peaked at #60 on Billboard’s Hot R&B Singles chart, staying on the chart for 7 weeks. Also during 1992, Jive Records released a compliation album, “The Best of Glenn Jones.” In 1994, Glenn released his seventh album, “Here I Am” which peaked at #39 on Billboard’s R&B Albums chart. The lead single, “Round and Round” was a minor hit, peaking at #24 on Billboard’s Hot R&B Singles chart, staying on the chart for 20 weeks.
	The follow-up singles from the album: the title song (which peaked at #68) and “It’s Gonna Be Alright” (which peaked at #45) were moderately successful on the charts. In 1998, Glenn’s second compliation album, “Greatest Hits: Giving Myself To You” was released on the Razor & Tie record label. During that same year, Glenn released his eighth album, “It’s Time” which peaked at #51 on Billboard’s R&B Albums chart.
	The tracks released from the album, “Let It Rain” (which peaked at #65) and “Baby Come Home” (which peaked at #80) were moderately successful on the R&B charts. In 2002 (four years after the release of his previous album), Glenn released his ninth album, “Feels Good” which peaked at #88 on Billboard’s R&B Albums chart, but no tracks from the album made the charts. In 2006, Glenn released his tenth album, “Forever: Timeless R&B Classics” which peaked at #49 on Billboard’s R&B Albums chart. These days, Glenn continues to be active with his music career. In 2018, he and singer Regina Belle released the single, “Love By Design.”
	He is also the founder of his non-profit organization, The Love Jones Foundation (which was established in memory of his mother, Ella-Lee Jones) which provides educational programs to youth & music therapy to elderly people.
